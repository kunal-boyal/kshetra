# Phase 4: growth — the plan

**Status:** approved by Kunal on 2026-10-30 with all its recommendations (D109), after his decision to start phase 4 (D107). Each step is planned in detail before it starts, as in phases 2 and 3.

**Scope:** spec section 15 leaves phase 4 to customer demand. With no customer yet, Kunal chose all four groups of its candidates (D107), 18 capabilities in all. On 2026-10-31 Kunal took regional languages out (D116), leaving 17:
- spec 15's list;
- what the review of an outside demo added (D96);
- M14, payroll inputs, with no statutory deductions (D14).

**Exit criteria:** spec 15 sets none for this phase. Each step ends as earlier steps did:
- its tests: unit, integration, the cross-tenant contract and end-to-end with axe;
- English and Hindi;
- the demo content where the step adds records;
- the step's decision;
- `pnpm verify` before every merge.

## How it is ordered

Four waves. Each wave starts with what needs nothing from Kunal and ends with what waits on his input, so building never stalls on an answer.

1. **Finding and reading** (wave A): things every tenant uses daily, small and independent.
2. **People** (wave B): extensions of leave, approvals, expenses and attendance that the spec already describes as "version 2".
3. **Forms, evidence and a new industry** (wave C): the form engine and the field record.
4. **Commerce and connected systems** (wave D): the largest work. It needs real examples from Kunal: schemes, forecasts, liquidation, and an ERP test system.

One further capability carries a long lead time outside code, so it starts as soon as Kunal answers and is built when its input exists: **scheduled reports by email**, which need SES out of its sandbox for production; they work locally with Mailpit.

## Decisions Kunal makes before the steps that need them

1. **The order.** *Recommended:* the waves as above, A1 first. Each wave is independent, so any can move up if a customer asks.
2. **ERP connectors (D5).** *Recommended:*
   - **Zoho Books first:** a cloud API with OAuth, testable on a free trial, reached through `publicFetch` on 443.
   - **Tally next, by file exchange:** Tally exports and imports XML, and a customer's Tally sits on their own machine, which the cell can't reach. A scheduled upload, or later a small agent the customer runs, keeps the cell's outbound traffic on 443 (D101).
   - **SAP only for a customer who needs it:** its interfaces differ by edition.
3. **Schemes (D1).** *Recommended:* the four kinds above (percentage, flat, slab, free goods), no stacking of two schemes on one line in the first version, and the best scheme applied for the customer automatically.
4. **The prototype's features that phase U left out** (D104): collections, monthly claims, send back, invited users, delivery dates, email codes, form drafts on the web and fix times. *Recommended:* not in this plan. Each can join a wave by its own decision when a customer asks.

## What is not in phase 4

- PF, ESI and other statutory deductions (D14).
- Receipt reading (OCR) for expenses: the spec places it after version 1, and no step here needs it.
- Custom domains for tenants (D105).
- Languages beyond English and Hindi (D116): taken out on 2026-10-31.
- The real-world exit checks of phases 1 to 3. They stay in progress.md's open items and run as soon as the company's accounts exist (D107).

## Size

17 capabilities in about 29 pull requests, at the pace of phases 2 and 3. Wave A is about a week; waves B and C two to three weeks each; wave D depends most on Kunal's examples and the ERP choice.
