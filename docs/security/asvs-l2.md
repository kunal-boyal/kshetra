# ASVS 5.0 Level 2 review

The product's self-review against the OWASP Application Security Verification Standard 5.0.0, Levels 1 and 2 (spec 13.1, decision D101). It is phase 3 step 10c, and the starting point for the external penetration test, whose scope pack is [penetration-test.md](penetration-test.md) (step 10d).

**Scope.** The cell API with its worker and scheduler, the directory and its sign-in, the vendor console's API, the web portal, the console, the field app's talk with the API, and the AWS infrastructure in `infra/`. Out of scope: AWS's own services, the identity providers customers bring, and the phone's operating system.

**Method.** Each requirement was checked against main as it stood on 2026-10-26, chapter by chapter, with the files that show it. What was missing was then fixed, documented or accepted in the pull requests below; each row says which. The evidence for requirements met at the review points to lines as they were then.

**Result.** Every requirement is met, not applicable, or an accepted risk. None was rated high when reviewed.

## Accepted risks

On 2026-10-27 Kunal chose that every requirement ends met, not applicable or a reasoned accepted risk. The risks he accepted himself are dated; the others are proposed by this review and stand once he merges it. Look at them all again before general availability and whenever what they rest on changes.

| Risk | Requirements | Why it is accepted | What limits it |
|---|---|---|---|
| SMS codes are the primary factor | V6.3.3, V6.6.1 | Field people sign in with nothing but a phone, often shared or low-end; an app-based factor for them would cost more sign-ins than it stops. Kunal, 2026-10-26 | Device binding, no access-changing permission on phones, an authenticator required for admins and open to anyone, per-number caps, the warning at Sign-in security (docs/security/authentication.md) |
| Plain HTTP from the load balancer to the tasks | V12.3.1, V12.3.3 | The hop never leaves the VPC; TLS to the task would need a private CA and certificates in every task for no exposure it removes. Kunal, 2026-10-27 | Private subnets; the tasks accept port 3000 only from the load balancer's security group; TLS 1.3 or forward-secret 1.2 up to the load balancer |
| The RDS certificate bundle is trusted process-wide | V12.3.4 | `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS` is how Node trusts a private CA for drivers that open their own sockets; the bundle names only Amazon's RDS roots. Proposed | The bundle is pinned by checksum in the image; every outbound call to an address a tenant names goes through the outbound guard to public addresses |
| No allowlist of outbound hosts | V13.2.5 | An egress proxy for a handful of providers would add a service to run and to fail. Kunal, 2026-10-26 | Egress only on 443 and 465; tenant-named addresses checked and connected to as checked; destinations listed in docs/services.md |
| No antivirus scan of uploads | V5.4.3 | The files accepted are JPEG photos, PNG, JPEG or WebP logos and CSV or XLSX imports; none is run, and each is decoded or parsed before use. Proposed | JPEG decode and checksum for photos, rejected photos never served, logos re-encoded, imports parsed and never served (spec 12.2) |

Also decided, and documented rather than accepted as a gap: database passwords are long-lived and rotated yearly and when anyone with access leaves (docs/security/keys-and-crypto.md), and sessions are not limited in number (docs/security/authentication.md).

## Dynamic scans

`tools/zap/scan.sh` runs OWASP ZAP (pinned by digest) against the API and the portal on this machine: the baseline scan of the portal, and the API scan of every route in the OpenAPI document, once without a token and once as a manager on a phone. Its findings and what was done with them are in [zap.md](zap.md). The scans are run by hand before each release and before the penetration test; they are not in `pnpm verify`, as the active scan takes longer than the rest together.
